# right-door-rear-window: intake research (2026-10-12)

From general knowledge of vehicle glazing (no web pages copied):
- Rubber-gasket (H-section / zip-strip) glazing: old buses, trucks, VW T2, Land Rover Series. Glass sits in a fat black rubber ring that wraps the sheet-metal edge; no metal frame visible. In ours: a thick black rubber ring round the donor hole, the cut metal edge hidden inside it, matte low-roughness-contrast rubber.
- Caravan / RV bolt-on window: an aluminium (or painted) frame plate outside, a clamp ring inside, screws all round. In ours: a flat pale-grey aluminium flange ring with a screw ring on the cabin face, dull oxidised shader.
- Chrome-trim car window (1970s saloon): a thin bright trim strip in a rubber channel. In ours: a dull, pitted chrome bead; risks reading clean.
- Cut-in by hand (Mad Max style): the hole flame-cut into the donor leaf, the glass held by welded angle-iron tabs; reveal is raw cut steel with slag. In ours: no frame at all, ragged reveal, weld tabs. Echoes the donor leaf's existing welds (`rear_door_donor_welds.gd`).
Take: the gasket or the bolt-on frame gives the clearest "different vehicle" read at shot size; the flame-cut look repeats the leaf's own cut and reads as "same builder", not "different vehicle".
