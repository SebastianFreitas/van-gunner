# rear-window-seal · intake research (2026-10-13)

## How real van rear-door windows are glazed
- **Bonded (modern vans: Sprinter, Transit, most since the 90s).** Glass sits on the *outside* of the door skin, held by a hidden urethane bead between the glass and a pinch flange. From the street: glass (often with a black ceramic frit border), a thin black edge or none. From the cabin: the bare cut edge of the inner pressed skin, sometimes with a thin U-shaped edge trim (Trim-Lok style), and the back of the frit/bead through the glass. No rubber fills the hole wall. Sources: patents.justia.com/patent/5746859 (urethane is usual on van rear doors; older designs used an encapsulating crown hiding the glass edge); van-window vendors (nomadicsupply, campervan-hq) on edge molding over the raw cut.
- **Gasket-set (older vans: VW T2, Bedford, old Transit Mk1/2).** One H-section rubber clamps the skin's single pinch flange in one channel and the glass in the other. What shows outside is a thin lip (5-10 mm) over the glass and the skin; inside, a similar thin lip on the flange edge. The rubber is only as deep as the pinch flange, not the whole door thickness; the inner pressed skin is held back from the hole by its own pressed return. Corners are moulded and slightly bulkier (autorestorer.com gasket install; forum note that tight corners need moulded radii and rubber shrinks with age).
- **What we take:** whichever system, the rubber/bead is a thin line at the glass on the street side; the cabin side shows steel (the inner skin's pressed edge or a thin trim), never a fat rubber tube through the hole.

## In ours
- Today `WindowGasket` is an H ring INWARD 0.08 / OUTWARD 0.04 spanning z_cab to z_street (rear_window_gasket.gd:10-22, 81-95): the whole hole wall and a wide cabin band are rubber. That is the "dumb rubber all around".
- Directions: (a) bonded look: glass moved to the street face, thin black bead/frit at its edge, cabin side shows the steel cut edge with a thin edge trim; (b) thin gasket: keep an H only at the street skin (depth ~ one flange), visible lip ~0.01-0.015 m, slightly fuller moulded corners, steel hole wall/inner skin edge visible from the cabin.
