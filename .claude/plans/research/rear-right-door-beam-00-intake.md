# rear-right-door-beam: intake research (2026-10-13)

Sources are general reference knowledge (no web fetch this round); no art copied.

- **Panel-van barn doors (Ford Transit, Mercedes Sprinter, VW Crafter):** the first-opening
  leaf carries the lock: a centre handle turns two vertical rods that end in cams or hooks
  engaging a keeper in the header and one in the floor; the second leaf has an internal pull
  latch. Take: the lock is a rod run along the meeting edge, keepers top and bottom.
- **Shipping-container doors:** the classic lock bar: round rods in welded guide brackets, a
  flat-bar handle with a retainer catch and a seal/padlock hole, cam keepers welded at both
  ends. Take: reads instantly as "lock", every piece is a separate welded/bolted bit, fits the
  redneck brief better than a hidden latch.
- **Farm gate / barn hardware:** barrel slide bolts, drop latches, a hasp with padlock and a
  chain loop. Take: cheap scavenged lock pieces bolted crooked onto a beam.
- **Mad Max: Fury Road war-rig doors:** heavy flat straps bolted across seams, mismatched bolt
  heads, weld beads left lumpy. Take: the straps "holding the yellow part" are short flat bars
  with 2 visible screws each, not one tidy row.
- **Truck body builders' corner posts:** a box or channel section welded along a cut door
  edge to stiffen it, ends mitred or bent round. Take: the beam is the door's new latch edge,
  bent along the existing 45-degree top cut.

What the code already gives (anchors in the plan's Current state): the bottom box beam (fresh
steel), the header's centre latch box (a natural top keeper), the scrap striker/hasp/padlock
at 36 % height, the angle-iron cut strips the beam would replace.
