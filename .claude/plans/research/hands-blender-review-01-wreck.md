# hands-blender-review 01: the wrecked fingers (D20, owner: you decide)

Brief research, 2026-10-18, on badly healed finger breaks, for the left index and left ring (D19).

- **Angular malunion of a phalanx.** A transverse phalanx fracture with more than 10 degrees of angulation, 2 mm of shortening or any rotation is operated on (StatPearls, Phalanx Fracture); malunions past 25 degrees affect both flexion and extension (OrthoBullets review). Untreated, the bent segment stays bent: in the coronal plane it reads as a finger that turns sideways at one joint. We take: 30 degrees sideways at the last joint, past the 25 degree "clearly deformed" line, claw following the tip.
- **Mallet finger.** The extensor slip at the last joint tears, the last joint droops toward the palm and cannot straighten; untreated it can drift into a swan neck (OrthoBullets). Cadaver models of apex-volar malunion: 16, 27, 46 degrees of angulation gave 10, 24, 66 degrees of lag; 40 degrees gave about 42 degrees (OrthoBullets evidence 9708382, Eaton hand ebook). We take: a 40 degree droop of the last joint toward the palm, no swan neck (it would move the middle joint too).
- **Boxer's fracture** (5th metacarpal neck): sunk knuckle and a slight claw; not used, since the knuckle row is the plan's widest feature and the knuckle heads grow 8 %.
- **Rotational malunion** (scissoring when curled): not used; a twist about a round tube does not read in a still view.

Choice: left index = sideways (radial) angular malunion, 30 degrees toward the thumb at the last joint; left ring = mallet droop, 40 degrees toward the palm at the last joint. Two different breaks so the four fingers still tell apart (Pass test 2). Effects worked out in phase 4: tip length along the bone x cos 30 = 0.866 (index) and x cos 40 = 0.766 (ring), which `arms touch` sees as shorter tips on the left hand (off the gun).

Sources: https://www.statpearls.com/kb/viewarticle/43245 · https://orthobullets.com/evidence/9708382 · https://eatonhand.com/ebook/id25_m.htm
